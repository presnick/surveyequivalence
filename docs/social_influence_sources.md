# Sources for independent and socially influenced estimates

The four suggested papers contain usable evidence, but study different forms of social information. Becker et al. (2017) is the closest match to the proposed candy-jar comparison: it includes real candy estimates, actual information exchange, and a separate independent control. None of these experiments directly tests people shouting answers aloud.

## Becker et al. (2017)

The experiment recruited 1,360 people into groups of 40: 13 decentralized networks, 13 centralized networks, and eight independent control groups. Each person was asked for three answers per task. Network participants saw peers' average answers between submissions; controls received no social information. The study appendix identifies six candy-jar tasks, `3a`–`3f`; the released data give true counts 279, 259, 268, 291, 278, and 278. A smaller panel in a reanalysis is a subset of recorded answers from an original 40-person group. It does not represent an experiment rerun with fewer interacting people. [Paper and methods](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1615978114); [study materials](https://ndg.asc.upenn.edu/experiments/collective-intelligence/).

## Lorenz et al. (2011)

This experiment had 144 participants in twelve sessions of twelve, answering six factual questions over five rounds. The information conditions varied by question within sessions. The available reanalysis CSV includes session, subject, task, truth, all five estimates, confidence, and information condition. It contains 852 participant-task rows, so it should not be described as a complete 864-row release without checking exclusions. Participants and question trials within a session are dependent. The paper's accuracy analysis uses transformed estimates, and its findings about reduced diversity should not be relabeled as a universal increase in raw arithmetic-mean error. [Original paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3107299/); [available CSV](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/amaatouq/task-dependence/master/data/prior_research_data/lorenz2011.csv).

## Kao et al. (2018)

The 659-object jar dataset contains 602 real paired responses: `GUESS 1`, `GUESS 2`, displayed value `S`, and claimed social group size `N`. However, the displayed number was computer-generated relative to each participant's initial estimate, then presented as an average of earlier guesses. These are observed human responses to manipulated information, not interacting group estimates. The independent datasets for other jar sizes do not have paired revisions. The paper's repeated-round social-influence curves are simulations and should not be presented as observed survey results. [Experimental methods and results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5938592/); [supplement collection](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.c.4064342).

## Almaatouq et al. (2022)

This paper combines theoretical analysis, simulation, and reanalysis of four published experimental datasets totaling 2,885 subjects. Its repository includes respondent-level files for Lorenz (2011), Gurcay (2015), Becker (2017), and Becker (2019), with scripts documenting harmonization. The study emphasizes that network centralization and the initial distribution of estimates jointly affect accuracy; social influence can help or hurt. These reanalyses are not four newly collected experiments. Use the original experiment's release and definitions when available, keep simulated results separate, and retain session/network identifiers when constructing panels or uncertainty estimates. [Paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9530231/); [repository](https://github.com/amaatouq/task-dependence).
